C. Introduction to Shortwave Diathermy

The term diathermy means "through heating", i.e. producing deep heating directly in the tissues of the body.

Externally applied heat sources such as hot towels, infrared lamps and electric heating pads may cause discomfort and skin burns before sufficient heat reaches deeper tissues.

In diathermy, the body becomes part of the electrical circuit and heat is produced within the body rather than being transferred through the skin.

Advantages:

🔥 Deep heating of tissues
🎚️ Controlled treatment intensity
📍 Localisation through electrode placement
⚙️ Adjustable heating via circuit parameters

D. Principle of Shortwave Diathermy

SWD uses high-frequency alternating current to produce heating within body tissues. Key parameters from the experiment record:

27.12 MHz
Operating frequency
≈ 11 m
Wavelength

The alternating current constantly reverses direction in the tissue. Tissue resistance to this rapidly changing current converts electrical energy into heat (Joule heating), so the heat is generated inside the tissue between the electrodes.

E. Types of Electrode Arrangement

The arrangement used in this experiment places two condenser electrode pads around the joint to be treated (knee). The electrodes sandwich the treatment region.

Visual reference: knee electrode placement figure in the experiment record. No other electrode arrangements are presented as part of this experiment.

G. Circuit Description

1. Oscillating Circuit

  • Transformer T1 steps up the mains to supply the valve filament and the high anode voltage.
  • Triode valve acts as the oscillator's active element, sustaining high-frequency current.
  • Tank (resonance) circuit: coil AB in parallel with condenser C1 sets the frequency.
  • Feedback coil CD returns part of the energy to the grid, generating positive feedback.

2. Patient Circuit

  • Coil EF couples energy from the oscillator by magnetic induction.
  • Variable capacitor C2 tunes the patient resonator.
  • Patient resonator must be tuned to the oscillator for maximum transfer.
  • Tuning indicator (meter) peaks at the tuned position.
  • Patient electrodes deliver the energy into the tissue.

Technical details from the record: anode supply ≈ 4000 V; maximum machine power 500 W. C2 is adjusted for maximum meter reading during tuning. A thermal delay stops current flowing until the valve filament reaches adequate temperature.

H. Intensity Control

Current supplied to the patient can be regulated by three methods:

1. Anode voltage
Varying the anode supply changes output.
2. Filament current
Varying filament heating changes electron emission.
3. Grid bias (grid leak R)
